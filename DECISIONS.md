# Decisions

Routine implementation decisions made while building *Room for Trouble* from
`docs/DESIGN_BRIEF.md`. Each entry says what was decided, why, and where it
lives. Where a decision departs from the brief, the departure is called out.

## Owner direction

**D1 — Overcooked-style perspective camera instead of the brief's orthographic camera.**
The owner asked for "a pixel style 3D game in the camera angle of Overcooked" and invited
design changes that fit better. The gameplay camera is a fixed perspective rig with a
53° pitch and a 30° field of view. It auto-fits the active floor bounds, so the whole floor
stays visible at every aspect ratio (`src/view/pixel_stage.gd`). It never rotates or follows
one player, which preserves the brief's "shared screen, no split" rule.

**D2 — Pixel-art rendering of a real 3D scene.**
The 3D world renders into a low-resolution SubViewport, by default 360 lines ("Classic"), and
is integer-upscaled with nearest filtering. Shading uses a toon shader with dithered light
bands. Forward+ adds a screen-space outline pass; the Compatibility renderer drops it.
Settings → Vision → Pixel size offers Chunky (270), Classic (360), Fine (540) and Off (native).
UI and captions render at native resolution, so text stays readable at handheld size.

## Engine and architecture

**D3 — Godot 4.7.2 stable, standard build, typed GDScript.**
The engine is pinned to 4.7.2. `tools/build.sh` refuses any other version. There is no C#,
GDExtension or third-party addon, so a fresh checkout plus the official templates builds.

**D4 — One pure, integer, fixed-tick simulation for every mode.**
`HotelSim` (`src/sim/hotel_sim.gd`) steps at 30 Hz on a plain dictionary state. Positions are
integer micrometres and time is integer ticks. Systems are static modules: move, interact,
guests, env, director, score, goals, Gus, tutorial and playground. The same code runs local
couch play, the authoritative online service, tests, the reference bot and replays. Rendering
and audio only read `sim.s` and `sim.events`.

**D5 — Determinism and replay.**
Mechanical and cosmetic random numbers come from separate xorshift streams. A mechanical
state hash is recorded every 5 action seconds. `SimReplay` (`src/sim/sim_replay.gd`) stores
the setup, the accepted input frames and the external commands (start, presence,
back-to-lobby, find tools, leader wrap, Gus). Re-running a record reproduces every
checkpoint, and the first mismatching checkpoint names the divergence window. The last
local campaign or Quick Shift is written to `user://replays/last_shift.json`.

**D6 — Content is data, generated from editable sources.**
Data lives in JSON:
- `data/campaign_manifest.json`, copied verbatim from Appendix A
- guests, items and stations
- layouts and templates
- tutorials, modifiers and cosmetics

Story scenes, localization, per-level schedules and credits are generated by
`tools/build_content.py` from `tools/content/strings_en.py` and `tools/content/story_src.py`.
Level purposes and silly payoffs are read straight from the design brief, so the prose has
one source.

**D7 — Online play uses WebSocket (WSS behind a TLS proxy) and a JSON protocol on one fixed RPC path.**
Clients send intents and per-seat input frames. The service validates each against a strict
schema (type, fields, sizes and value ranges) and seat ownership. Rooms snapshot to their
own peers only, with section-level deltas and a full state every 2 s. The protocol uses no
Godot object decoding.

**D8 — There is no client-side prediction.**
Remote clients interpolate authoritative snapshots about 100 ms behind. The hotel is cosy,
not twitchy, and prediction would be a second source of truth for item ownership. Local
input feedback (highlights, glyphs and audio) is immediate.

**D9 — Online story beats play locally.**
Each client plays pre-shift and postcard scenes on its own screen. The server's per-seat
"planning ready" gates the shift start, so a fast skipper never starts the shift early for
others.

## Gameplay details the brief left open

**D10 — Auto Catch defaults to on.** Handoffs then work for younger players without a timing
window. It can be turned off per seat.

**D11 — The welcome kiosk has unlimited gifts.** Gifts are a matching puzzle, not a stock
puzzle. Stock pressure lives in towels, snacks and linen.

**D12 — The thermostat and kiosk cycle with Work; there is no radial selector.** Repeated
Work presses step through settings or gifts, with an icon preview. This avoids a second
radial (the ping radial) and keeps one button meaning "use this".

**D13 — Planning "ready" uses the Hint button (LB / R).** Carry and Work stay free for
rearranging rooms during planning.

**D14 — Playground board operations mutate the local sim directly.** The Playground is a
sandbox, and its "demonstrate each family" board spawns guests and items there.

**D15 — There is no spectator mode.** A late joiner waits for the next welcome (the next
shift) instead of watching. Joining an in-progress shift is refused with "underway".

**D16 — P5 lift pad moved to (1, 10).** The brief's coordinate clashed with a pedestal.

**D17 — Staff handoff targeting needs at least 0.25 m separation, plus the facing cone.**
A teammate standing on top of you is never an ambiguous handoff target. Before this change,
two overlapping players could hand an item back and forth by accident.

**D18 — Bots are reference drivers, not playtesters.** `src/dev/reference_bot.gd` plays
without Quick Step, guest helpers or co-carry. Its scores are a floor, and the docs never
present them as family playtests.

## Presentation

**D19 — All art is procedural, from code.** Actors, stations, items and props are built from
primitive meshes in code (`src/view/models/*`, `src/view/mesh_kit.gd`) with one shared
palette (`src/view/art.gd`). This needs no binary art pipeline, keeps a consistent style,
and puts every mesh under version control. Icons are 16-colour text-pixel sources
(`data/art/icons/*.txt`), routed through the palette and high-contrast variants at runtime.

**D20 — All audio is original procedural synthesis.** `tools/audio/synth.py` (NumPy) renders
music, stings, effects and the "babble" voices to Ogg Vorbis. The output is committed, so
building the game needs no Python audio dependencies.

**D21 — Fonts are Pixelify Sans for headings and Nunito for body text (both SIL OFL 1.1).**
Settings → Vision → Readable font switches all text to Nunito.

**D22 — Story scenes are staged on the real hotel set.** Scenes use the same builder,
stations and furnished rooms as gameplay, with mess hidden. They use posed actors, a small
set of camera presets and per-level props. Captions carry speaker names. Read-aloud uses
the platform's text-to-speech when enabled.

**D23 — Shift-list blurbs are player-facing.** The designer "purpose" lines stay in the data
for tools. Players see short, plain blurbs (`level.Lxx.blurb`).

## Operations

**D24 — The dedicated server is a Godot headless export of the same project.** Its
`--server` mode uses the same `Content` hash, so mismatched clients are refused at `hello`
("version"). A `--probe URL` mode performs a bounded protocol health exchange. Docker's
healthcheck uses it, and it reports an aggregate room count, never codes or identities.

**D25 — Deployment uses Docker Compose with Caddy for TLS.** The compose file pins image
digests. The game container is read-only, has no capabilities and runs as a non-root user.
Nothing is provisioned or published automatically (§22.5). The owner chooses the domain
and host.

**D26 — There are no secrets in the repository.** No API keys are required. `.env` is
gitignored and `.env.example` documents every variable.

**D27 — The client endpoint is configurable three ways.** In order of precedence:
`RFT_ENDPOINT` per launch, an `endpoint.txt` beside the executable (an operator-provided
default), or Settings → Online. Private TLS CAs work through `RFT_TLS_CA_FILE` with
verification on. `RFT_ALLOW_INSECURE_TLS=1` exists only for local testing.

## Performance

**D28 — Static station bodies are merged; actors are single skinned meshes.**
- Every station's static body is baked into one floor mesh at load. The original nodes stay
  hidden, so they can still be outlined for interaction highlights.
- Each character's parts are baked into one skinned mesh whose bones follow the animation
  pivots (`ActorRig.bake`). Parts toggled on their own, such as slippers and the mask, stay
  separate.
- Only main body masses cast shadows.

Together these cut the busiest floor from 622 to about 154 draw calls with the identical
look. Per-station flattening was tried and rejected: it defeated Godot's automatic
instancing of identical station meshes.

**D29 — A "Low" graphics preset.** Settings → Vision → Graphics → Low turns off the sun's
shadow pass and the outline pass. That keeps the busiest floor under the brief's
150-draw-call low-preset budget (131 measured), for integrated GPUs and handhelds.

**D30 — The macOS export is ad-hoc signed with hardened runtime.** The previous export kept
the engine template's broken signature, so Gatekeeper would report a downloaded copy as
"damaged". Ad-hoc signing makes the bundle valid, though Gatekeeper still asks for "Open
Anyway". Distribution signing and notarization need a paid Developer ID, which this machine
lacks (it has only an Apple Development identity).
